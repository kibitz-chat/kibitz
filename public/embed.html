Sunday Stew, the Long Way

A recipe you make together, even apart · 3 hr · serves 6

Some recipes are instructions; this one is an afternoon. You will brown things too slowly, argue gently about bay leaves, and at some point someone will say "it needs more salt" without tasting it. That someone is why the call button exists on this page.

Cooking the same dish in two kitchens over a call is the closest thing to sharing one. The pot can't travel — the kibitzing can.

You'll need

Two pounds of stewing beef, patience, a heavy pot, an onion you'll cry about, four carrots, a friend on the other end who insists their grandmother did it differently. Red wine optional but historically inevitable.

The way

Brown the beef in batches — crowding the pot is the only sin this recipe recognizes. Sweat the onion in what's left behind. When your friend's onion finishes first, let them gloat; theirs will be underseasoned and you both know it. Deglaze, return the beef, cover with stock, and now: two hours of low simmering, which is exactly what the floating call window is for. Cards, crossword, or just company while two pots tick away in two cities.

Carrots in the last forty minutes. Taste before salting. Serve to whoever is in the room — and to whoever is in the call, in spirit.
